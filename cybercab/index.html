Local ownership. A national Cybercab network.

The proposed first markets are the Hartford-area suburbs in Connecticut and suburban New Jersey. Each branch reaches 200 Cybercabs before the next one opens.

The askThe 200-vehicle gate

The branch

  • A lot, a small office, a wash bay, and charging.
  • Guests book an autonomous trip. Not a self-drive rental.
  • Cybercab only. The local owner holds the branch.

Assumptions cover supply, permission, insurance, and the grant.

About thirty seconds

A local yard for every Cybercab that has to come home.

Between trips a Cybercab needs a charge, a clean cabin, an inspection, and a place to wait. That is the operating problem. A centralized depot can serve one dense city from far away. A branch is a small yard in the community — lot, office, wash bay, and charging — run by someone who lives with the result.

The hypothesis is that local yards keep vehicles nearer the next trip and make empty miles visible, where a distant fleet yard can hide them. It is a hypothesis. A pilot would test it. Nothing here treats it as already shown.

The owner would hold the branch: equity, control, and a share of profit. A first-time owner grant is proposed to make that possible. It is not funded, and no amount is stated.

The proposed pilot is suburban: the Hartford-area suburbs in Connecticut, and suburban New Jersey. One branch fills to 200 Cybercabs on site before another opens. A first launch in about seven weeks is conditional on vehicle access, insurance, local permission, premises, and financing.

Support this proposal asks to discuss

  1. Vehicle access for one pilot branch.
  2. An operational partnership: the branch runs the yard; Tesla keeps the autonomy and the booking.
  3. How pilot financing would work, including the proposed owner grant.

There is no dollar ask on this page, and no unit economics. Tesla has not agreed, endorsed, or funded any of it.

01 — Location

A yard that can hold the whole branch.

The first site would be sought in the Hartford-area suburbs in Connecticut or in suburban New Jersey. The search looks for a lot with a path to 200 stalls, a small office, a wash bay, and enough power to charge. Ready-to-use property is preferred, so a utility rebuild does not become the thing the calendar waits on.

What the search checks

  1. Stay inside one of the two proposed first markets. The proposal does not rank them.
  2. Choose a town only when a real search is underway.
  3. Tour lots with room for 200 stalls, and prefer 220 or more as buffer.
  4. Confirm a usable office, a wash bay or space to build one, and the power service.
  5. Seek a letter of intent, with a pilot exit if the property owner will give one.
  6. Keep a ready-to-use backup in the same market.

A site drops out if flooding or crime cannot be managed, or if the rent cannot be carried. Rent is a local number, set when a property is actually in view.

Fit-out

  • Lighting and cameras, before guests come onto the lot.
  • Rows for Ready, Charging, Wash, Quarantine, and Maintenance.
  • Wash equipment only with a discharge permit.
  • Charge stalls only after the utility signs off.
  • An office with first aid, protective equipment, a tablet for the logs, and a locked file.

Site go / no-go

A branch opens when every line below is yes. One no keeps it closed. Spending on improvements that only make sense once vehicles arrive waits for a written supply path. A backup stays in the same market.

02 — Owners

A local owner, vetted before they hold the branch.

Two decisions stay separate: whether someone can run the yard, and whether a proposed grant would fund them. This site describes that path. It does not take names or applications.

1. The owner record

A candidate would be asked for a legal name, an entity, the market they would serve, whether they would be a first-time majority owner, relevant operations experience, a working-capital range, agreement to fill one branch to 200 before a second site, and any other businesses. The record does not ask for a government ID number or a bank password.

2. A score, then a decision

Each line is scored from 1 to 5. Counsel may change the criteria. The total is 35. The decision is Advance, Hold, or Decline.

  1. 1. First-time owner verification/ 5
  2. 2. Rural commitment/ 5
  3. 3. Operations learning ability/ 5
  4. 4. Working-capital readiness/ 5
  5. 5. Background outlook, with counsel/ 5
  6. 6. Training completion plan/ 5
  7. 7. Understands there is no guaranteed income/ 5
  8. Total/ 35

3. Due diligence before a key

  • Counsel chooses the screening vendor and the package for each role.
  • Packages differ for lot staff and for an owner.
  • The path is offer, consent, screen, decision, with adverse action handled by counsel.
  • Screening starts at least two weeks before launch, longer when counsel requires it.
  • No unsupervised access to the lot before a clear decision.
  • People are not hired to drive Cybercabs. Offers that require driving them stop.

4. Training before solo work

A new person shadows one opening and one closing. A safety module that is failed is remediated before a retest. There is a practical dry run on an empty lot before the first live vehicle. Unsupervised access waits for a sign-off.

  • Module ALot safety and emergency
  • Module BInspect and photo standard
  • Module CWash and cabin clean
  • Module DCharging etiquette and stall faults
  • Module EStage the Ready row
  • Module FIncident intake and escalation
  • Module GPrivacy, lot cameras, and the cabin camera

5. The grant decision is a different gate

Passing training does not award money. The proposed grant, described further down, would fund milestones only after legal, tax, and franchise review. It is not a hiring bonus, not a vehicle loan, and not an existing program. Expectations for anyone recruited are physical wash work, weather, and photo discipline, with no guaranteed finish time on a peak day, no promise of Tesla employment, and no utilization bonus the founder cannot fund.

03 — Setup

Stand the company up before the first vehicle arrives.

The sequence is deliberate: a legal entity, then banking, payroll, insurance, screening, accounting, and training. Vehicle supply is a written path, agreed with Tesla, before any Cybercab is ordered or advertised.

Entity

Counsel chooses the form — limited liability company or corporation, as advised. The legal name is cleared so it does not use Tesla, Cybercab, or Robotaxi. Articles, an employer identification number, a registered agent, and an operating agreement are the evidence. Trademark confusion stops the naming.

Vehicle supply

The ask is a meeting on Cybercab access and a single pilot branch. Vehicles are ordered only after that path is written. A conversation is not a reason to build fleet-only improvements.

Insurance

A broker would be asked to quote commercial general liability, property, workers’ compensation, and auto or garagekeepers cover as applicable, including whatever commercial Cybercab terms Tesla and the insurer require. The branch does not open to guests without that binder.

Local permission

Business license as the locality requires. Zoning for staging. A wash-water discharge permit. An electrical permit if the charge hardware needs one. Operating permission is its own yes, separate from the lease.

People

The founder selects sites, recruits operators, holds the standard, owns the scorecard and the expansion gate, designs the grant proposal, and would be the liaison if Tesla agrees to talk. A center owner runs hiring, coverage, daily readiness, incidents, and the branch’s own books. Shift leads open and close. Cleaners wash and help inspect. Technicians watch stalls and the lot. They do not drive the vehicles. The first paid shift waits until workers’ compensation and payroll are live.

Systems

Day to day, the branch keeps a simple record: fleet status, inspections, charge and wash sessions, incidents, the roster, and a weekly note from the owner to the founder. It is a yard record, not a dispatch system. How a branch would be paid is a settlement question for the partnership.

Banking and bookkeeping stay in a business account, separate from personal spending. An accountant and a payroll vendor are chosen with counsel.

05 — Daily work

The branch charges, cleans, and releases. It does not drive.

Published hours would follow local Robotaxi demand once Tesla shared the pattern. Until then there is no published schedule. Peak days — travel weekends, storms, local events — would add wash and charge coverage. Holidays follow local labor law. A week’s shifts are posted by Thursday. The lot does not open without a named shift lead.

  1. 01

    Open

    Unlock the office and the lot. Confirm lights and cameras. Count Ready, Charging, Wash, Quarantine, and Maintenance. Read overnight incidents. Name who owns each bay and stall. Confirm which charge stalls are online. A failed camera or gate is escalated before anyone is accepted onto the lot.

  2. 02

    Wash

    Exterior wash, interior wipe and vacuum, trash out. Use soap that is safe for sensors. Never cover the cabin camera or change the vehicle’s software. A wastewater backup or a chemical spill stops the bay.

  3. 03

    Inspect

    An eight-point exterior photo set, a look for tire, body, and leak problems, a clear cabin camera, a clean interior, and an empty trunk. Charge must meet the center’s floor. Any brake, leak, or structural doubt goes to Quarantine with a ticket. Only a pass reaches Ready.

  4. 04

    Charge and stage

    Move a vehicle only by a method Tesla has authorized. Improvised driving is prohibited. Log the stall and the state of charge at both ends. Stow the cable. Face the Ready row toward the exit. A stall that faults twice in a day is stopped.

  5. 05

    Incident

    Injuries or crime go to local emergency services first. Photograph, quarantine, write the record, notify the founder on the same shift. An immobilized vehicle off the lot waits for Tesla-authorized recovery. Informal cash settlements for injuries are refused.

  6. 06

    Close

    The physical count matches the register. Quarantine is locked. The wash bay and chemicals are secured. Lost property is logged at the branch. Keys are inventoried. The office is locked.

Until Tesla states a minimum charge, the branch uses 60 percent as its own floor. Maintenance tickets run Safety, then Ops, then Cosmetic, and they close only after a re-inspect passes.

06 — Scorecard and the 200 gate

Two hundred on site, then the next branch.

A full parking layout is not the milestone. The next branch opens when 200 Cybercabs are actually on site, the scorecard has been green for four weeks, insurance is current, and no critical incident is open. The branch has also been operating for at least four weeks. Any no keeps the next branch closed. Parking for 200, vehicles on site, and vehicles that can turn in a day are three different numbers.

Ready percent

Ready units divided by units on site. Counted daily.

Clean first pass

Releases that did not need a rewash, divided by cleans. Daily.

Charge availability

Stalls up, divided by stalls installed. Daily.

Mean turn minutes

The inspect, wash, and charge cycle. Daily.

Fill ratio

Units on site divided by 200. Weekly.

Open safety tickets

Count of open tickets whose priority is Safety. Daily.

07 — Expansion

Rural America before the cities.

The ambition is a national network that reaches rural localities before it reaches cities. The proposed first markets are suburban: the Hartford-area suburbs in Connecticut, and suburban New Jersey. Later branches follow that rural-before-cities order. Cities come last. Every branch, wherever it sits, fills to 200 Cybercabs on site before another opens.

First

One branch in a proposed suburban market. The proposal names both markets and does not rank Hartford-area Connecticut ahead of suburban New Jersey, or the reverse.

Then

Rural localities, each with the same yard, the same owner path, and the same 200-vehicle gate. Permission, power, and wash rights are local every time.

Last

Cities. The proposal does not name a year for them, or a count of branches along the way.

Requirements

What has to be true before a branch opens.

The traffic idea is a hypothesis. A pilot would measure it, including empty miles and induced demand, which can move congestion the wrong way. The proposal does not claim a cure.

No-go list

  • No written path to Cybercab supply
  • No operating permission in the locality
  • No insurance binder for the branch
  • No safe, powered site with wash rights
  • No trained lead to open and close
  • Using the checklists as if they dispatched vehicles

Alongside the list

Settlement terms, working capital, charging capacity, wash rights, zoning, and a written operating territory. The seven-week sketch moves if any of them slip. The 200-vehicle rule does not.

Traffic, measured in the pilot

The ambition is to ease traffic jams. A pilot would publish a local baseline and the same figures once Cybercabs are running, using data Tesla and the municipality agree on. Empty repositioning miles are part of that count.
