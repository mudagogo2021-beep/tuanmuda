Keeping NYC's federal SNAP dollars in city-owned stores

SNAP benefits are paid by the federal government. In FY2025, $5.04 billion in SNAP benefits went to New York City households[4]. Today that money is spent at private stores, and most U.S. SNAP dollars end up at big supermarkets and super stores[2]. Mayor Zohran Mamdani's N.Y.C. Groceries plan creates five city-owned stores. Every SNAP dollar spent there is federal money landing in a store the City owns.

5 stores, one per borough
All open by end of the Mayor's first term; first at Hunts Point (Bronx) by late 2027, La Marqueta (East Harlem) by 2029.[7][8]
$70M capital budget
For construction and fit-out; $30M of it for the ground-up La Marqueta store.[9]

All produce, meat and seafood plus ~20 staple categories, for everyone. City owns the site; a private operator runs the store.[7][9]

About the "700,000" figure: City data shows about 1.6 million NYC SNAP recipients in about 979,000 households (August 2026), down from about 1.8 million people in 2024–25[5][4]. The closest published number to 700,000 is the roughly 690,000 households that get SNAP without cash assistance (HRA, September 2026)[5].

    Why there are no company names here

    An earlier draft of this app listed per-company SNAP figures (for example "Walmart $29.46B") and credited them to "USDA FNS FY2025 SNAP Retailer Reports." No such per-company report exists. Those numbers have been removed. USDA does not publish how much SNAP money any company or store takes in, and the Supreme Court let it keep that secret in 2019 (see tab 3).

    The 5-tier idea (mega-chains → regional chains → corporate pharmacy/dollar/convenience → small independents → single-store bodegas) sorts stores by who owns them. USDA only sorts by format, so the tiers can't be filled with public data. That gap is the problem the draft amendment (tab 5) would fix.

    Why you can't see how much SNAP money each store gets

    Taxpayers fund SNAP benefits, but how much each store or chain takes in is secret. The grocery industry fought in court and in Congress to keep it that way.

    1. 2011: A newspaper asks
      The Argus Leader (Sioux Falls, SD) files a Freedom of Information Act request for every store's annual SNAP redemptions, FY2005–2010. USDA refuses under FOIA Exemption 4, and the paper sues.[10]
    2. 2016–2018: The paper wins, the grocery lobby steps in
      A federal court orders the data released, and the Eighth Circuit upholds the order. USDA stops defending the secrecy. The Food Marketing Institute (FMI), the grocery retailers' trade association, intervenes and appeals.[10][11] In January 2017, USDA warned retailers their store-level redemption totals were about to be released under court order.[13]
    3. 2018: Industry tries a farm bill rider
      The House version of the 2018 farm bill carried language to exempt grocers' SNAP data from FOIA. The National Newspaper Association objected, and the final farm bill dropped it. The National Grocers Association called protecting store-level SNAP sales data "a top priority" and promised to keep working with Congress on it.[11]
    4. June 24, 2019: Supreme Court sides with FMI
      Food Marketing Institute v. Argus Leader Media, 588 U.S. 427 (2019), decided 6–3. The Court ruled that commercial information is "confidential" under Exemption 4 if the business keeps it private and the government promised privacy. A business no longer has to show disclosure would cause it substantial competitive harm. Store-level SNAP data stays secret.[10][12]
    5. Since then
      USDA publishes only state totals and store-format breakdowns. Congress could override the ruling by changing the law[12], but we could not find any enacted law or currently moving bill that makes store-level SNAP redemption data public. (The 2026 "SNAP Data Transparency and Oversight Act," S.3856, is about states sharing recipient data with USDA, not store sales.[14])
    Why this matters for NYC: Without store-level data, the City can't see how much of its residents' $5 billion a year in SNAP goes to national chains versus local stores, or measure how much its own stores win back. A city-owned store could publish its own SNAP sales, because no one can keep that secret from the owner.

    Proposed amendment (draft, not law)

    Draft amendment to 7 U.S.C. § 2018(c)

    This is advocacy draft text. It has not been introduced in Congress and is not current law. It would require USDA to publish store-level SNAP redemptions for chains, overriding the Argus Leader outcome for them, while protecting single-store bodegas by reporting them only as ZIP-code totals.

    PROPOSED AMENDMENT (DRAFT, NOT LAW)

A BILL
To amend section 9(c) of the Food and Nutrition Act of 2008 (7 U.S.C. § 2018(c)) to establish a standardized 5-tier classification framework for Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) authorized retailers, mandate mandatory disclosure of retail redemption totals for corporate conglomerates, and protect the commercial privacy of single-store independent bodegas.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,

SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.
This Act may be cited as the "SNAP Retail Transparency and Municipal Food Access Act of 2026".

SEC. 2. DISCLOSURE OF SNAP RETAIL REDEMPTION DATA BY CORPORATE CLASSIFICATION.
Section 9(c) of the Food and Nutrition Act of 2008 (7 U.S.C. § 2018(c)) is amended—
  (1) by striking "Regulations issued" and inserting "(1) IN GENERAL.—Regulations issued"; and
  (2) by adding at the end the following:

  "(2) MANDATORY DISCLOSURE AND 5-TIER RETAIL CLASSIFICATION.—
      "(A) DEFINITIONS.—In this subsection:
          "(i) TIER 1: NATIONAL GROCERY AND MASS-MARKET MEGA-CONGLOMERATES.—The term 'Tier 1 retailer' means any retail food store entity operating nationwide or across multiple geographic regions with an aggregate corporate footprint exceeding 1,000 locations or annual SNAP redemptions exceeding $100,000,000, including parent companies, subsidiaries, and banners (e.g., Walmart Inc., The Kroger Co., Costco Wholesale, Albertsons Companies, Ahold Delhaize USA, Amazon/Whole Foods, ALDI U.S., Target Corporation).
          "(ii) TIER 2: REGIONAL SUPERMARKET CHAINS.—The term 'Tier 2 retailer' means any supermarket company operating 10 or more retail locations or generating $10,000,000 or more in annual SNAP redemptions, regardless of family or private ownership status (e.g., Publix Super Markets, H-E-B, Wakefern Food Corp./ShopRite, WinCo Foods, Meijer, Wegmans Food Markets, Big Y World Class Market, Demoulas Market Basket, Hy-Vee). Tier 2 retailers shall under no circumstances be classified as small or independent retailers.
          "(iii) TIER 3: NATIONAL AND REGIONAL NON-GROCERY CORPORATE CHAINS.—The term 'Tier 3 retailer' means any corporate pharmacy chain, dollar store chain, or corporate convenience store chain operating 10 or more locations nationwide or regionally (e.g., CVS Health, Walgreens Boots Alliance/Duane Reade, Dollar General, Dollar Tree/Family Dollar, 7-Eleven Inc., Wawa, Circle K, Sheetz, Cumberland Farms).
          "(iv) TIER 4: SMALL MULTI-STORE INDEPENDENT GROCERS.—The term 'Tier 4 retailer' means any independent grocery entity operating between 2 and 9 retail locations with annual SNAP redemptions under $10,000,000.
          "(v) TIER 5: SINGLE-STORE INDEPENDENTS, BODEGAS, AND FARMERS MARKETS.—The term 'Tier 5 retailer' means any single-location neighborhood bodega, corner grocery store, independent deli, or farmers market.

      "(B) PUBLIC DISCLOSURE OBLIGATIONS.—
          "(i) TIERS 1, 2, AND 3.—The Secretary shall publish on an annual basis, in a machine-readable format accessible to the public, the total dollar amount of SNAP benefits redeemed at each authorized store location, summarized by parent company, brand banner, and ZIP code for all Tier 1, Tier 2, and Tier 3 retailers. Such data shall not be exempt from public disclosure under section 552(b)(4) of title 5, United States Code (commonly known as Freedom of Information Act Exemption 4), notwithstanding any judicial interpretation under Food Marketing Institute v. Argus Leader Media, 139 S. Ct. 2356 (2019).
          "(ii) TIER 4.—Redemption data for Tier 4 retailers shall be disclosed publicly summarized by ZIP code and store format.
          "(iii) TIER 5 BODEGA PRIVACY PROTECTION.—To safeguard small independent corner stores from unfair competitive practices by national conglomerates, store-specific redemption data for Tier 5 retailers shall remain confidential and shall be disclosed exclusively as an aggregated ZIP-code total combining all Tier 5 locations in that postal code.

  "(3) MUNICIPAL CIRCULATION SUPPORT.—States and municipalities operating municipal grocery programs or non-profit food cooperatives shall have full access to Tier 1, Tier 2, and Tier 3 store-level redemption data for urban food planning and SNAP circulation analysis."
